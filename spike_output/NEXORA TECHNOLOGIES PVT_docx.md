**NEXORA TECHNOLOGIES PVT. LTD.**

**EMPLOYEE HANDBOOK**

**Handbook Version:** 2.0 **Effective Date:** 1 August 2026 **Document Owner:** People &amp; Culture Department **Applies To:** All regular employees of Nexora Technologies Pvt. Ltd.

**Welcome to Nexora**

Welcome to Nexora Technologies Pvt. Ltd.

Nexora is a technology company focused on building software products and digital solutions for businesses across multiple industries. We believe that our people are central to our success and are committed to providing a professional, inclusive, collaborative, and respectful workplace.

This Employee Handbook provides employees with information about the company's workplace policies, procedures, benefits, expectations, and employee responsibilities.

The handbook is intended to serve as a practical reference for employees throughout their employment with Nexora.

**1. Purpose of the Employee Handbook**

The purpose of this handbook is to provide employees with a clear understanding of Nexora's employment policies and workplace practices.

The handbook covers areas including:

- Working hours and attendance
- Leave and holidays
- Remote and hybrid work
- Compensation and payroll
- Employee benefits
- Workplace conduct
- Information security
- Performance management
- Employee grievances
- Disciplinary procedures
- Resignation and separation

Employees are expected to read and understand the policies applicable to their role.

**2. Scope and Applicability**

Unless a policy specifically states otherwise, the policies in this handbook apply to all regular full-time and part-time employees of Nexora Technologies Pvt. Ltd.

Certain policies may have different requirements for:

- Employees on probation
- Interns and trainees
- Contract employees
- Employees working in client locations
- Employees assigned to international projects

Where a specific employment agreement, applicable law, or formally issued company policy provides a different requirement, the applicable agreement, law, or policy will take precedence.

**2.1 Using This Handbook**

This handbook is intended as a general guide and does not contain every rule or procedure applicable to employees.

Employees should refer to the relevant policy section before making decisions related to leave, attendance, expenses, company equipment, information security, or other employment matters.

For questions that are not addressed in this handbook, employees may contact the People &amp; Culture Department through the official employee support channel.

**2.2 Policy Updates**

Nexora may review and update its policies when business requirements, operational practices, or applicable laws change.

Employees will be informed of material policy changes through official company communication channels.

The latest officially published version of the Employee Handbook should be treated as the current version.

**2.3 Important Notice**

This handbook is a company policy reference document and should not be interpreted as a complete employment contract.

Nothing in this handbook overrides applicable laws or an employee's signed employment agreement.

Employees are responsible for complying with the policies applicable to their employment and role.

**3. Employment Basics**

**3.1 Employment Categories**

Nexora Technologies Pvt. Ltd. classifies employees into the following categories:

| **Employment Category**    | **Description**                                                                                              |
|----------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Regular Full-Time Employee | An employee working the standard full-time schedule under a regular employment agreement.                    |
| Regular Part-Time Employee | An employee working fewer hours than the standard full-time schedule under a part-time employment agreement. |
| Probationary Employee      | A newly hired employee undergoing the company's initial assessment period before confirmation.               |
| Intern                     | A student or recent graduate engaged for a defined learning and work experience period.                      |
| Trainee                    | An individual engaged in a structured training program before being assigned to a regular role.              |
| Fixed-Term Employee        | An employee hired for a specified period under a fixed-term employment agreement.                            |
| Contract Employee          | An individual engaged through a third-party staffing or contracting organization.                            |

The benefits, leave entitlements, working arrangements, and other employment conditions may differ depending on the employee's employment category.

Where a policy applies only to a particular employment category, the applicable category will be stated explicitly.

**3.2 Joining and Onboarding**

All newly hired employees must complete the company's onboarding process.

The onboarding process may include:

1. Submission and verification of required employment documents.
2. Creation of company email and system accounts.
3. Completion of mandatory HR and compliance forms.
4. Completion of information security and data protection training.
5. Introduction to the employee's department and reporting manager.
6. Completion of role-specific onboarding activities.

Employees are expected to provide accurate and complete information during onboarding.

Failure to provide required documentation may delay onboarding activities or access to company systems.

**3.3 Probation Period**

Newly hired regular employees are generally placed on probation for **six months** from their date of joining.

The probation period is intended to allow Nexora and the employee to evaluate:

- Job performance
- Technical and functional capabilities
- Attendance and punctuality
- Workplace conduct
- Ability to work effectively with the team
- Adherence to company policies

The reporting manager may provide feedback during the probation period and may recommend confirmation, extension of probation, or other appropriate action.

**3.4 Extension of Probation**

The standard probation period may be extended when additional time is required to evaluate an employee's performance or suitability for the role.

A probation extension may be recommended for reasons including:

- Performance concerns
- Insufficient opportunity to evaluate the employee
- Extended absence during the probation period
- Significant changes in role or responsibilities

Any extension will be communicated to the employee through an official company communication.

The employee will receive the revised probation end date and, where applicable, expectations that must be met during the extended period.

**3.5 Confirmation of Employment**

An employee who successfully completes probation may be confirmed as a regular employee.

Confirmation is not automatic solely because the probation period has ended. The employee must receive formal confirmation from the company.

The confirmation decision may consider performance, conduct, attendance, and feedback from the employee's reporting manager.

Employees should retain their confirmation communication for their employment records.

**3.6 Employee Information and Records**

Employees are responsible for ensuring that their personal and employment information maintained by Nexora is accurate and up to date.

Employees should promptly inform the People &amp; Culture Department of changes to information such as:

- Residential address
- Personal contact number
- Emergency contact
- Bank account information
- Government identification information
- Educational qualifications
- Marital or dependent information where required for benefits administration

Employees must not knowingly provide false, misleading, or fraudulent information to the company.

**3.7 Internal Transfers**

Employees may apply for or be considered for transfer to another department, team, project, or company location based on business requirements and available opportunities.

An internal transfer may require:

- Approval from the current reporting manager
- Approval from the receiving manager
- Verification of the employee's eligibility
- Completion of the internal transfer process

Approval of a transfer is subject to business requirements and is not guaranteed solely because an employee meets the stated eligibility criteria.

The effective date of an approved transfer will be communicated to the employee.

**3.8 Changes in Job Role**

Nexora may modify an employee's responsibilities based on business requirements, organizational changes, project requirements, or career development considerations.

Where a substantial change to an employee's role affects their employment terms, the company will communicate the applicable changes through the appropriate formal process.

Employees are expected to cooperate with reasonable changes in responsibilities that fall within the scope of their employment.

**3.9 Work Location**

Employees are assigned a primary work location when they join Nexora.

Depending on business requirements, employees may be required to work:

- From a designated Nexora office
- Under an approved hybrid arrangement
- From a client location
- From another company-approved work location

Employees must comply with the work-location requirements applicable to their role and team.

Working from a location that has not been approved by the company may require prior authorization from the employee's reporting manager and, where applicable, the People &amp; Culture Department.

**3.10 Employee Identification**

Each employee is assigned a unique Employee ID during onboarding.

The Employee ID may be used for:

- HR records
- Attendance systems
- Internal applications
- Payroll-related processes
- Employee support requests
- Access to company facilities

Employees must not share their employee credentials or authentication information with another person.

**3.11 Attendance During Probation**

Employees on probation are expected to meet the same attendance and working-hour requirements applicable to their assigned role unless otherwise specified in their employment agreement or applicable policy.

Repeated unexplained absence, late attendance, or failure to follow attendance procedures may be considered during the probation review.

**3.12 Employment Agreements**

Employees will receive an employment agreement or appointment documentation specifying the terms applicable to their employment.

The employment agreement may contain information such as:

- Job title
- Compensation
- Employment category
- Probation period
- Notice requirements
- Primary work location
- Benefits and applicable conditions

Employees should review their employment agreement carefully and contact the People &amp; Culture Department if they require clarification regarding their employment terms.

**3.13 Background Verification**

Nexora may conduct background verification before or after an employee joins the company, subject to applicable laws and company procedures.

Verification may include validation of information such as:

- Educational qualifications
- Previous employment
- Professional certifications
- Identity documents
- Other information relevant to the employee's role

Employees are expected to cooperate with legitimate verification requests.

Material misrepresentation or submission of fraudulent documents may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment, subject to applicable law.

**3.14 Employee Responsibilities**

Every employee is expected to:

1. Perform assigned responsibilities professionally and responsibly.
2. Follow company policies and procedures.
3. Maintain accurate personal and employment information.
4. Protect company property and confidential information.
5. Maintain professional workplace conduct.
6. Complete mandatory training within the required timelines.
7. Report workplace concerns through appropriate channels.
8. Cooperate with reasonable company processes and requests.

Failure to meet these responsibilities may result in corrective or disciplinary action depending on the circumstances.

**3.15 Questions Regarding Employment Status**

Questions regarding an employee's employment category, probation status, confirmation, transfer, or employment records should be directed to the People &amp; Culture Department.

Employees should refer to their individual employment agreement for terms that are specific to their employment.

Where this handbook and an employee's signed employment agreement contain different provisions, the applicable employment agreement will govern the employee's contractual terms, subject to applicable law.

**4. Working Hours and Attendance**

**4.1 Standard Working Hours**

The standard working schedule for full-time employees at Nexora Technologies is **40 hours per week** , generally distributed across five working days from Monday to Friday.

The standard office working hours are:

**9:30 AM to 6:30 PM**

Employees are generally expected to be available during their assigned working hours unless they have an approved alternative work arrangement.

Working hours may vary for employees who:

- Work in shifts
- Support international clients
- Work at client locations
- Have an approved flexible working arrangement
- Are assigned to projects requiring different schedules

The applicable schedule will be communicated by the employee's reporting manager.

**4.2 Working Days**

The standard working week consists of five working days.

Saturday and Sunday are normally non-working days unless an employee is assigned to a shift, client project, production support activity, or another business requirement that requires weekend work.

Employees required to work on a scheduled non-working day will be informed by their reporting manager in advance wherever reasonably possible.

**4.3 Attendance Recording**

Employees must record their attendance using the attendance system designated by Nexora.

Depending on the employee's work arrangement, attendance may be recorded through:

- The company's attendance portal
- An approved employee application
- Office access systems
- Another system designated by the company

Employees are responsible for ensuring that their attendance records are accurate.

Employees must not record attendance on behalf of another employee or allow another person to record attendance on their behalf.

**4.4 Daily Attendance Requirement**

Employees are expected to report to work and complete their assigned working hours on scheduled working days.

Employees working from an office are expected to be physically present at their designated workplace unless they have an approved remote or hybrid arrangement.

Employees working remotely must remain available through approved communication and collaboration tools during their scheduled working hours.

Failure to remain available during scheduled working hours without an approved reason may be treated as an attendance concern.

**4.5 Punctuality**

Employees are expected to begin work at their scheduled start time.

For employees following the standard schedule, the expected start time is **9:30 AM** .

Occasional delays caused by unforeseen circumstances should be communicated to the reporting manager as soon as reasonably possible.

Repeated late arrival may result in a discussion with the reporting manager and may be considered during performance or attendance reviews.

**4.6 Grace Period**

Nexora provides a **15-minute grace period** for occasional delays in arrival.

For employees following the standard 9:30 AM start time, arrival by **9:45 AM** will generally fall within the grace period.

The grace period is intended for occasional unforeseen delays and should not be treated as a revised daily start time.

Repeatedly relying on the grace period may be considered a punctuality concern.

The grace period does not eliminate the employee's responsibility to complete the required working hours.

**4.7 Missed Attendance Entry**

If an employee forgets to record attendance, the employee must submit an attendance correction request through the designated attendance system.

The request should normally be submitted within **two working days** of the missed entry.

The employee's reporting manager may be required to approve the correction.

Repeated missed attendance entries may be reviewed by the employee's manager or People &amp; Culture Department.

**4.8 Absence Reporting**

Employees who are unable to attend work must inform their reporting manager as soon as reasonably possible.

Where the absence is planned, the employee should obtain approval in advance according to the applicable leave policy.

For unexpected absence, the employee should notify the reporting manager before the scheduled start of work whenever reasonably possible.

Failure to report an absence may result in the absence being treated as unauthorized.

**4.9 Breaks**

Employees are entitled to reasonable breaks during the working day.

Under the standard schedule, employees generally receive:

- One meal break of up to **60 minutes**
- Short personal breaks as reasonably required

Employees should coordinate breaks with their team when necessary to ensure that business operations and customer commitments are not affected.

Break periods should not be used to regularly extend the working day beyond the scheduled end time.

**4.10 Flexible Working Hours**

Certain employees may be eligible for flexible working hours depending on their role and team requirements.

Flexible working arrangements must be approved by the employee's reporting manager.

Approval may depend on factors including:

- Team coverage
- Client requirements
- Business operations
- Employee responsibilities
- Meeting schedules

Flexible working hours do not automatically reduce the employee's required weekly working hours.

**4.11 Overtime**

Employees may occasionally be required to work beyond their normal working hours due to urgent business requirements, production incidents, client commitments, or critical project deadlines.

Where overtime approval is required, employees must obtain approval from their reporting manager before performing planned overtime.

Compensation or time-off arrangements for overtime will depend on the employee's employment category, applicable company policy, and applicable law.

Employees should not assume that working additional hours automatically creates an entitlement to overtime compensation.

**4.12 Weekend and Holiday Work**

Employees may occasionally be required to work on weekends or company holidays due to business requirements.

Where reasonably possible, the employee's manager will communicate such requirements in advance.

Eligible employees may receive compensatory time off or other applicable benefits in accordance with company policy and applicable law.

Employees should consult their reporting manager or People &amp; Culture Department regarding eligibility for compensation or compensatory time off.

**4.13 Shift-Based Work**

Employees assigned to shift-based roles must follow the shift schedule communicated by their reporting manager.

Shift schedules may include:

- Morning shifts
- Evening shifts
- Night shifts
- Rotational shifts

Employees are expected to report for their assigned shift on time.

Requests to exchange shifts with another employee require approval from the appropriate manager before the change takes effect.

**4.14 Attendance During Remote Work**

Employees working remotely are expected to maintain the same level of professional availability and accountability as employees working from the office.

Remote employees must:

- Be available during scheduled working hours.
- Attend required meetings.
- Respond to work-related communications within a reasonable time.
- Maintain a suitable working environment.
- Follow company information security requirements.
- Use approved company systems for work activities.

Remote work does not mean that employees may freely change their working hours without approval.

**4.15 Unapproved Absence**

An absence may be considered unauthorized when an employee:

- Does not report to work without informing the company.
- Fails to obtain required leave approval.
- Does not provide required documentation for an absence.
- Remains absent after approved leave has ended without authorization.

Unauthorized absence may result in loss of pay and/or disciplinary action depending on the circumstances and applicable policy.

**4.16 Extended Absence**

Employees who expect to be unavailable for an extended period should inform their reporting manager and People &amp; Culture Department as early as possible.

The employee may be required to provide supporting documentation depending on the reason for the absence.

For extended leave, employees should ensure that important responsibilities are appropriately handed over before the leave begins whenever reasonably possible.

**4.17 Attendance and Performance Reviews**

Attendance and punctuality may be considered as part of an employee's overall performance and conduct assessment.

Attendance alone will not determine an employee's performance rating.

However, repeated unexplained absence, habitual late arrival, failure to follow attendance procedures, or prolonged unavailability may result in corrective action.

**4.18 Attendance Disputes**

Employees who believe their attendance record is incorrect should first raise the issue through the designated attendance correction process.

If the issue is not resolved, the employee may contact the People &amp; Culture Department.

Employees should retain relevant supporting information, such as approved leave requests or manager communications, when raising an attendance dispute.

**4.19 Attendance Responsibilities**

Employees are responsible for:

1. Recording attendance accurately.
2. Following their assigned working schedule.
3. Informing their manager about unexpected absence or delays.
4. Obtaining approval for planned leave and schedule changes.
5. Correcting missed attendance entries within the required timeline.
6. Remaining available during scheduled working hours.
7. Following applicable remote-work and attendance procedures.

Managers are responsible for ensuring that attendance-related concerns are handled consistently and fairly within their teams.

**5. Leave and Holidays**

**5.1 Overview**

Nexora Technologies provides eligible employees with paid and unpaid leave to support personal needs, health, family responsibilities, and planned time away from work.

Employees are expected to use leave responsibly and follow the applicable approval and documentation procedures.

Leave entitlements may vary depending on an employee's employment category, length of service, and applicable law.

Unless otherwise specified, leave is calculated based on the calendar year from **1 January to 31 December** .

**5.2 Types of Leave**

Nexora provides the following primary categories of leave:

| **Leave Type**    | **Annual Entitlement**   | **General Purpose**                             |
|-------------------|--------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|
| Annual Leave      | 18 days                  | Planned personal or recreational time off       |
| Casual Leave      | 6 days                   | Short-term personal requirements                |
| Sick Leave        | 10 days                  | Illness, medical appointments, or recovery      |
| Bereavement Leave | Up to 5 days             | Death of an immediate family member             |
| Parental Leave    | As applicable            | Birth or adoption of a child                    |
| Unpaid Leave      | Subject to approval      | Extended absence when paid leave is unavailable |

Additional leave may be provided where required by applicable law or a specific company policy.

**5.3 Annual Leave**

Eligible regular employees receive **18 days of Annual Leave per calendar year** .

Annual Leave may be used for planned personal activities, vacations, family commitments, or other personal reasons.

Employees should generally submit Annual Leave requests at least **five working days in advance** .

Approval is subject to business requirements and team availability.

**5.4 Annual Leave Carry-Forward**

Employees may carry forward unused Annual Leave into the following calendar year.

A maximum of **10 unused Annual Leave days** may be carried forward.

Any Annual Leave balance exceeding the permitted carry-forward limit will expire at the end of the calendar year unless applicable law or a specific company policy provides otherwise.

Example:

An employee has 14 unused Annual Leave days on 31 December.

- 10 days may be carried forward.
- The remaining 4 days will expire.

Employees are encouraged to plan their leave throughout the year rather than accumulating leave unnecessarily.

**5.5 Annual Leave During the Joining Year**

Employees who join Nexora during the calendar year receive a prorated Annual Leave entitlement.

The entitlement is calculated based on the employee's joining date and the remaining portion of the calendar year.

Employees should refer to their HR portal for their specific leave balance.

**5.6 Casual Leave**

Eligible employees receive **6 days of Casual Leave per calendar year** .

Casual Leave is intended for short-term personal requirements that do not require extended time away from work.

Examples may include:

- Attending to urgent personal matters
- Family commitments
- Personal appointments
- Unexpected short-term requirements

Casual Leave should generally not be used for extended planned vacations.

Employees should request Casual Leave in advance whenever reasonably possible.

**5.7 Casual Leave Rules**

Casual Leave:

- Cannot normally be carried forward to the following calendar year.
- Cannot normally be converted into Annual Leave.
- Requires manager approval when advance approval is reasonably possible.
- May be subject to a minimum or maximum duration based on operational requirements.

Unused Casual Leave at the end of the calendar year will normally lapse.

**5.8 Sick Leave**

Eligible employees receive **10 days of Sick Leave per calendar year** .

Sick Leave may be used when an employee is:

- Ill or recovering from an illness.
- Attending a medical appointment.
- Required to rest following medical advice.
- Unable to perform their normal duties due to illness.

Employees should inform their reporting manager as soon as reasonably possible when they are unable to work due to illness.

**5.9 Medical Documentation**

Nexora may request medical documentation when required to verify an extended or repeated period of Sick Leave.

A medical certificate may be requested when an employee takes **more than three consecutive working days of Sick Leave** .

Employees must submit requested documentation through the designated HR process within the specified timeline.

The company will handle employee medical information in accordance with applicable privacy requirements.

**5.10 Sick Leave Carry-Forward**

Unused Sick Leave does not normally carry forward to the following calendar year.

Unused Sick Leave will lapse at the end of the calendar year unless applicable law requires a different treatment.

Sick Leave cannot normally be exchanged for cash.

**5.11 Emergency Leave**

Employees may take leave for unexpected personal emergencies when immediate absence from work is necessary.

Examples include:

- A serious family emergency
- An unexpected incident requiring immediate personal attention
- Urgent situations that cannot reasonably be planned in advance

Employees should inform their reporting manager as soon as possible.

Emergency leave may be adjusted against an applicable leave balance depending on the circumstances.

**5.12 Bereavement Leave**

Employees may receive up to **5 working days of paid Bereavement Leave** following the death of an immediate family member.

Immediate family members generally include:

- Spouse
- Parents
- Children
- Siblings

Additional leave may be considered based on individual circumstances and applicable law.

Employees should inform their reporting manager or People &amp; Culture Department as soon as reasonably possible.

Documentation may be requested where required for administrative purposes.

**5.13 Parental Leave**

Eligible employees may receive parental leave following the birth or adoption of a child.

Parental Leave includes:

- Maternity Leave
- Paternity Leave
- Adoption-related leave

The duration and eligibility requirements for Parental Leave will be determined in accordance with applicable law and the company's current parental leave policy.

Employees should contact People &amp; Culture before the expected leave period to understand the applicable entitlement and documentation requirements.

**5.14 Unpaid Leave**

Employees who require time away from work after exhausting applicable paid leave may request Unpaid Leave.

Unpaid Leave is not an automatic entitlement and requires approval.

Approval may depend on:

- Reason for the request
- Duration of absence
- Business requirements
- Employee's performance and attendance record
- Available leave balances
- Applicable employment terms

Employees should submit Unpaid Leave requests as early as possible.

**5.15 Leave During Probation**

Employees on probation may be eligible for leave according to the applicable leave policy and their employment terms.

However, employees should understand that taking leave during probation may affect the evaluation period where the absence significantly limits the company's ability to assess performance.

Where necessary, the probation period may be extended in accordance with the Employment Basics policy.

**5.16 Leave Approval**

Submitting a leave request does not automatically mean that the leave has been approved.

Employees must wait for the required approval before taking planned leave.

Leave requests may be declined or modified when business requirements make the requested period unavailable.

Employees should not make non-refundable travel or other commitments based solely on a submitted leave request.

**5.17 Unplanned Absence**

When an employee is unable to provide advance notice because of an unexpected situation, the employee should notify their reporting manager as soon as reasonably possible.

The employee should submit the appropriate leave request after the immediate situation has been addressed.

Failure to communicate an absence may result in the absence being treated as unauthorized.

**5.18 Combining Different Types of Leave**

Different leave categories may be combined when permitted by company policy and approved by the appropriate authority.

For example, an employee may be permitted to combine Annual Leave with Sick Leave when the relevant eligibility and documentation requirements are satisfied.

Employees should ensure that each leave category is recorded correctly in the HR system.

**5.19 Public and Company Holidays**

Nexora observes a designated holiday calendar each year.

The holiday calendar may include:

- National holidays
- Public holidays
- Regional holidays
- Company-designated holidays

The applicable holiday calendar may vary depending on the employee's assigned work location.

Employees should refer to the official holiday calendar published by People &amp; Culture for the applicable year and location.

**5.20 Holidays Falling During Leave**

A company holiday that falls within an employee's approved period of Annual Leave will **not normally be deducted from the employee's Annual Leave balance** .

For example, if an employee takes Annual Leave from Monday through Friday and Wednesday is a designated company holiday, only four Annual Leave days will normally be deducted.

Different rules may apply to other leave categories where specified by company policy or applicable law.

**5.21 Leave During Notice Period**

Employees serving a notice period may request leave in accordance with the applicable leave policy.

Approval of leave during the notice period is subject to business requirements and the employee's manager.

Leave taken during the notice period may affect the employee's final working date where required by the employee's employment agreement or applicable policy.

Employees should discuss extended leave during the notice period with People &amp; Culture before making arrangements.

**5.22 Leave Without Approval**

Employees must not assume that absence from work will be treated as approved leave.

Absence without required approval or communication may be treated as unauthorized absence.

Unauthorized absence may result in:

- Loss of pay
- Attendance records being adjusted
- Corrective action
- Disciplinary action in serious or repeated cases

**5.23 Leave Records**

Employees can view their leave balances and submitted requests through the designated HR system.

Employees are responsible for reviewing their leave records and reporting discrepancies to People &amp; Culture.

If an employee believes their leave balance is incorrect, they should raise the issue with supporting information such as previous approvals or leave records.

**5.24 Leave Encashment**

Annual Leave may be eligible for encashment only where specifically permitted by company policy, employment terms, or applicable law.

Casual Leave and Sick Leave are not normally eligible for encashment.

Employees should contact People &amp; Culture for the applicable rules regarding leave encashment at the time of separation or other eligible circumstances.

**5.25 Leave Responsibilities**

Employees are responsible for:

1. Planning leave in advance whenever possible.
2. Submitting leave requests through the designated HR system.
3. Obtaining approval before taking planned leave.
4. Informing their manager about unexpected absence.
5. Providing required documentation when requested.
6. Maintaining accurate leave records.
7. Ensuring appropriate work handover before extended planned leave.

Managers are responsible for reviewing leave requests fairly while considering business requirements, team availability, and applicable policies.

**5.26 Leave Policy Questions**

Questions regarding leave balances, eligibility, approval, carry-forward, documentation, or holidays should be directed to the People &amp; Culture Department.

Where an employee's employment agreement or applicable law provides a different entitlement from the general policy described in this handbook, the applicable agreement or legal requirement will take precedence.

**6. Remote and Hybrid Work Policy**

**6.1 Purpose**

Nexora Technologies supports flexible working arrangements where they are compatible with business requirements, employee responsibilities, client commitments, and information security requirements.

Remote and hybrid work arrangements are intended to provide flexibility while maintaining productivity, collaboration, service quality, and accountability.

A remote or hybrid arrangement is a work arrangement approved by the company and is not an unconditional employee entitlement.

**6.2 Types of Work Arrangements**

Nexora generally supports the following work arrangements:

**Office-Based**

Employees work primarily from their designated Nexora office or another company-approved workplace.

**Hybrid**

Employees divide their working time between an approved Nexora office and an approved remote location.

**Fully Remote**

Employees perform their assigned responsibilities remotely on a full-time basis where the role and business requirements permit such an arrangement.

The applicable arrangement depends on the employee's role, department, project, client requirements, and manager approval.

**6.3 Eligibility for Remote or Hybrid Work**

Remote or hybrid work may be available to employees whose roles can be effectively performed outside the office.

Eligibility may depend on:

- Nature of the employee's role
- Business and operational requirements
- Client requirements
- Team collaboration requirements
- Employee performance
- Attendance and reliability
- Information security considerations
- Availability of suitable remote-working infrastructure

Not all roles are eligible for remote or hybrid work.

Roles requiring physical presence, specialized equipment, direct customer interaction, or secure facilities may require employees to work from an approved office or client location.

**6.4 Standard Hybrid Arrangement**

Where a team follows a hybrid arrangement, employees may generally be required to work from the office **three days per week** and may work remotely for the remaining two working days.

The exact schedule may vary between teams.

Managers may designate specific office days based on:

- Team meetings
- Client requirements
- Collaboration needs
- Training activities
- Business events
- Project requirements

Employees should follow the schedule communicated by their reporting manager.

**6.5 Requesting a Remote Work Arrangement**

Employees who wish to work remotely or change their existing hybrid schedule should submit a request through the designated company process.

The request should include:

- Proposed work arrangement
- Requested duration
- Proposed remote location
- Reason for the request, where required
- Any relevant supporting information

Approval must be obtained before the requested arrangement begins.

Submitting a request does not constitute approval.

**6.6 Temporary Work From Home**

Employees may request temporary Work From Home when circumstances make office attendance difficult.

Examples may include:

- Short-term personal emergencies
- Temporary transportation difficulties
- Minor illness where the employee is able to work
- Home-related emergencies
- Other circumstances approved by the reporting manager

Temporary Work From Home should generally be requested before the start of the working day.

The reporting manager may approve, modify, or decline the request based on business requirements.

**6.7 Working Hours During Remote Work**

Employees working remotely must follow their assigned working hours unless an alternative schedule has been approved.

For employees following the standard schedule, the expected working hours remain:

**9:30 AM to 6:30 PM**

Remote work does not change the employee's expected working hours.

Employees must remain available through approved communication and collaboration platforms during working hours.

**6.8 Availability and Communication**

Employees working remotely are expected to maintain effective communication with their teams.

Employees should:

- Remain reachable during scheduled working hours.
- Attend scheduled meetings.
- Respond to important work communications within a reasonable time.
- Keep their work status updated when required.
- Inform their manager when they will be temporarily unavailable.
- Use approved communication platforms for company business.

Repeated unavailability during working hours may result in the remote work arrangement being reviewed.

**6.9 Remote Work Location**

Employees must work from a remote location that has been approved or permitted under the applicable work arrangement.

Employees should maintain a work environment that allows them to perform their responsibilities effectively and securely.

Employees must not regularly work from public locations where confidential company or client information could be exposed.

Examples of unsuitable locations may include:

- Public cafes
- Unsecured shared workspaces
- Public transportation
- Other locations where unauthorized individuals may access company information

Exceptions may be permitted for short periods where appropriate security controls are available.

**6.10 Working From Another City or Country**

**7. Compensation and Payroll**

**7.1 Compensation Overview**

Nexora Technologies provides compensation to employees based on their employment agreement, role, responsibilities, experience, qualifications, and applicable compensation structures.

The compensation package may consist of:

- Base salary
- Allowances
- Variable or performance-based compensation
- Eligible benefits
- Other components specified in the employee's employment agreement

**7.2 Salary Structure**

An employee's salary structure is communicated in their employment agreement or compensation statement.

The structure may include components such as:

| **Component**        | **Description**                                                                 |
|----------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Basic Salary         | Fixed component of an employee's compensation                                   |
| House Rent Allowance | Allowance provided where applicable under the employee's compensation structure |
| Special Allowance    | Fixed allowance determined by the employee's compensation structure             |
| Variable Pay         | Compensation linked to specified performance or business criteria               |
| Other Allowances     | Additional components applicable to specific roles or employees                 |

The exact composition of an employee's salary may vary.

Employees should refer to their individual compensation statement for their specific salary components.

**7.3 Payroll Cycle**

Nexora operates a monthly payroll cycle.

Employees are generally paid once per month.

Salary for a particular month is normally processed so that employees receive payment by the **last working day of that month** , subject to banking operations, public holidays, payroll processing requirements, and other circumstances that may affect the payment date.

The company may communicate changes to the payroll schedule when necessary.

**7.4 Salary Payment Method**

Salary is paid directly into the bank account registered with Nexora.

**7.5 Payslips**

The payslip generally contains information such as:

- Employee identification details
- Pay period
- Earnings
- Allowances
- Deductions
- Net salary
- Applicable statutory contributions

Employees should review their payslips regularly and report discrepancies promptly.

**7.6 Gross Salary and Net Salary**

Gross salary refers to the employee's earnings before applicable deductions.

Net salary refers to the amount credited to the employee's bank account after applicable deductions.

Deductions may include legally required or authorized amounts such as:

- Income tax
- Employee provident fund contributions
- Other statutory contributions
- Approved employee deductions
- Recoveries authorized under applicable policies

The difference between gross and net salary may therefore vary between employees.

**7.7 Statutory Deductions**

Nexora makes deductions required under applicable laws and regulations.

Depending on the employee's circumstances and applicable law, deductions may include:

- Income tax
- Provident fund contributions
- Professional tax
- Other statutory deductions

The company will apply statutory deductions based on the information and declarations available through the applicable payroll process.

- Bank account changes
- Tax declarations
- Approved allowances

- Approved deductions

Nexora Technologies expects all employees to conduct themselves with professionalism, integrity, respect, and accountability.

Employees are expected to comply with this policy regardless of whether they are working:

- From a Nexora office
- Remotely
- At a client location
- While travelling for business
- At a company event
- At another location while performing company-related work

**9.2 Professional Conduct**

Employees must perform their responsibilities honestly, professionally, and responsibly.

Employees are expected to:

1. Treat colleagues, clients, vendors, and visitors with respect.
2. Communicate professionally.
3. Perform assigned responsibilities diligently.
4. Follow applicable company policies.
5. Protect company and client interests.
6. Use company resources responsibly.
7. Raise concerns through appropriate channels.
8. Cooperate with legitimate workplace investigations.

Unprofessional or inappropriate conduct may result in corrective or disciplinary action.

**9.3 Respectful Workplace**

Nexora is committed to maintaining a workplace where employees are treated with dignity and respect.

Employees must not engage in behaviour that is intimidating, threatening, humiliating, abusive, or deliberately disruptive.

Respectful behaviour applies to:

- Verbal communication
- Written communication
- Email
- Messaging platforms
- Video meetings
- Social interactions at work
- Company events
- Client interactions

Employees are expected to consider the impact of their behaviour on others.

**9.4 Discrimination**

Nexora does not tolerate unlawful discrimination in the workplace.

Employment-related decisions should be based on legitimate factors such as:

- Qualifications
- Skills
- Experience
- Performance
- Business requirements
- Role suitability

Employees must not discriminate against another person in connection with employment or workplace activities on grounds protected by applicable law.

Concerns regarding discrimination should be reported through the appropriate grievance or reporting channel.

**9.5 Harassment**

Nexora prohibits harassment and other inappropriate conduct in the workplace.

Harassment may include unwelcome conduct that creates an intimidating, hostile, humiliating, or offensive work environment.

Examples may include:

- Repeated insulting comments
- Threatening behaviour
- Offensive jokes or remarks
- Unwelcome personal comments
- Deliberate humiliation
- Repeated unwanted communication
- Other conduct prohibited by applicable law

Nexora does not tolerate violence, threats of violence, or behaviour that creates a serious safety risk.

- Threaten another employee or third party.
- Engage in physical violence.
- Intentionally damage property.

- Use intimidation to influence workplace decisions.

**9.7 Bullying**

Bullying is not acceptable at Nexora.

Bullying may involve repeated unreasonable behaviour intended to intimidate, isolate, humiliate, or undermine another person.

Examples may include:

- Repeated personal insults
- Deliberate exclusion from necessary workplace communication
- Persistent humiliation
- Threatening or intimidating communication
- Repeated unreasonable behaviour intended to undermine an employee

Reasonable performance feedback, legitimate management decisions, and constructive criticism do not automatically constitute bullying.

**9.8 Conflicts of Interest**

Employees must avoid situations where their personal interests conflict, or appear to conflict, with Nexora's business interests.

Potential conflicts may include:

- Financial interests in a company that does business with Nexora
- Personal relationships that influence business decisions
- Outside employment that conflicts with Nexora responsibilities
- Participating in hiring decisions involving a close relative
- Using company information for personal benefit

Employees must disclose actual or potential conflicts to their reporting manager or People &amp; Culture.

**9.9 Outside Employment**

Employees must not undertake outside employment, consulting, freelancing, or business activities that:

- Conflict with their responsibilities at Nexora.
- Interfere with their working hours.
- Use Nexora resources or confidential information.

- Compete with Nexora's business.

Employees should disclose proposed outside professional activities when required by company policy.

**9.10 Gifts and Hospitality**

Employees must exercise caution when accepting or offering gifts, meals, entertainment, or other benefits from clients, vendors, suppliers, or business partners.

Employees must not accept or offer anything that could improperly influence a business decision.

Employees may accept modest and customary business gifts where:

- The gift is not cash or a cash equivalent.
- It is not intended to influence a business decision.
- It does not create an actual or perceived obligation.
- Acceptance is consistent with applicable company policy.

Cash gifts, gift cards, or other cash-equivalent benefits must not be accepted from vendors or business partners.

Employees should disclose questionable gifts to their manager or People &amp; Culture.

**9.11 Anti-Bribery**

Nexora prohibits bribery and improper payments.

- Customers
- Vendors
- Suppliers
- Business partners
- Government officials
- Other third parties

**9.18 Social Media**

**10. Workplace Harassment and Anti-Harassment Policy**

**10.1 Purpose**

Nexora Technologies is committed to providing a workplace where employees are treated with dignity and respect.

The company does not tolerate harassment, sexual harassment, discrimination, bullying, intimidation, or retaliation.

This policy applies to employees, interns, trainees, contractors, clients, vendors, and other individuals interacting with Nexora employees in a work-related context.

**10.2 Prohibited Harassment**

Harassment includes unwelcome behaviour that may reasonably create an intimidating, hostile, humiliating, or offensive work environment.

Examples may include:

- Offensive comments or jokes
- Repeated unwanted personal remarks
- Threatening or intimidating behaviour
- Unwelcome physical contact
- Offensive messages, images, or communications
- Deliberate humiliation or exclusion
- Repeated conduct intended to undermine another person

A single serious incident may also constitute harassment depending on the circumstances.

**10.3 Sexual Harassment**

Sexual harassment includes unwelcome conduct of a sexual nature, whether verbal, non-verbal, written, visual, or physical.

Examples may include:

- Unwelcome sexual comments
- Sexual jokes or messages
- Unwanted physical contact
- Requests for sexual favours
- Sharing sexually explicit material
- Unwelcome comments about a person's appearance
- Behaviour that creates a sexually hostile work environment

Employees should report concerns even if they are uncertain whether the conduct formally meets the definition of sexual harassment.

**10.4 Harassment Through Digital Platforms**

The anti-harassment policy applies to workplace-related digital communication, including:

- Email
- Company messaging platforms
- Video meetings
- Collaboration tools
- Work-related social media groups

Harassment does not become acceptable simply because it occurs online or outside the physical workplace.

**10.5 Reporting a Concern**

Employees who experience or witness harassment may report the concern through:

1. People &amp; Culture
2. Their reporting manager
3. The designated Ethics or Compliance contact
4. The designated complaints committee or confidential reporting channel, where applicable

Employees may report a concern even if they do not have complete evidence.

**10.6 Investigation**

Nexora will review reported concerns through an appropriate investigation process.

Depending on the circumstances, an investigation may involve:

- Interviews with relevant individuals
- Review of documents or communications
- Examination of other relevant evidence
- Consultation with appropriate internal or external specialists

Employees involved in an investigation are expected to cooperate honestly.

Information will be handled as confidentially as reasonably possible, subject to the requirements of the investigation and applicable law.

**10.7 Protection Against Retaliation**

Retaliation against an employee who raises a concern in good faith or participates in an investigation is prohibited.

Employees should report suspected retaliation through the same reporting channels available for harassment concerns.

**10.8 False or Malicious Complaints**

Employees are encouraged to report concerns in good faith.

A complaint will not be considered malicious merely because the allegation cannot be substantiated.

However, knowingly making a false complaint or deliberately providing false information during an investigation may result in disciplinary action.

**10.9 Support for Employees**

Employees who report or experience harassment may request information about available support resources through People &amp; Culture.

Depending on the circumstances, support may include:

- Employee Assistance Program resources
- Workplace accommodations
- Information about the complaints process
- Other appropriate support

**10.10 Policy Responsibilities**

Employees are expected to maintain respectful workplace behaviour and report serious concerns through appropriate channels.

Managers who become aware of a potential harassment concern must respond appropriately and escalate the matter according to company procedures.

Employees should not attempt to conduct their own investigation or confront an alleged offender where doing so could create additional risk.

**11. Information Security and Data Privacy**

**11.1 Purpose**

Nexora protects company, employee, customer, and client information from unauthorized access, disclosure, modification, loss, or destruction.

Employees are responsible for following information security and data protection requirements whenever they access company information or systems.

**11.2 Confidential Information**

Confidential information may include:

- Customer and client information
- Employee records
- Financial information
- Business strategies
- Source code
- Credentials
- Internal documents
- Product information
- Contracts
- Proprietary data

Employees must access confidential information only when authorized and required for their work.

**11.3 Passwords and Authentication**

Employees must:

- Use strong passwords where required.
- Keep passwords confidential.
- Never share authentication credentials.
- Use multi-factor authentication when required.
- Report suspected credential compromise immediately.

Employees must not write passwords in publicly accessible locations or use another employee's account.

**11.4 Company Devices**

Company devices must be protected against unauthorized access.

Employees must:

- Lock devices when unattended.
- Install security updates when required.
- Use approved security software.
- Report lost or stolen devices immediately.
- Avoid installing unauthorized software.

Employees must not disable security controls without authorization.

**11.5 Data Sharing**

Employees must share company or client information only with authorized recipients.

Confidential information must not be sent through personal email accounts, unauthorized cloud storage, or unapproved messaging applications.

Employees should verify recipients before sending sensitive information.

**11.6 Personal Data**

Personal data must be collected, accessed, stored, and processed only for legitimate business purposes and in accordance with applicable privacy requirements.

Employees should access personal information only when their role requires it.

Unnecessary copying or storage of personal data is prohibited.

**11.7 Phishing and Suspicious Messages**

Employees should be cautious of unexpected emails, messages, links, attachments, or requests for credentials or financial information.

Potential phishing attempts should be reported to the IT or Information Security team.

Employees should not knowingly forward suspicious messages to other employees without following the company's security reporting process.

**11.8 Security Incidents**

Employees must report suspected security incidents immediately.

Examples include:

- Lost company devices
- Accidental disclosure of confidential information
- Suspected malware
- Compromised credentials
- Unauthorized system access
- Phishing attacks
- Sending sensitive information to the wrong recipient

Employees should not attempt to conceal or independently investigate a suspected security incident.

**11.9 Remote Access**

Employees accessing company systems remotely must use approved devices, networks, authentication methods, and security tools.

Public or unsecured networks should not be used to access highly sensitive company information unless approved security controls are in place.

**11.10 Data Retention and Disposal**

Employees must retain company information only for the period required by business, legal, or policy requirements.

Confidential documents must be disposed of using approved secure disposal methods.

Employees must not permanently delete or destroy records that are subject to a legal, regulatory, or company retention requirement.

**11.11 Employee Responsibilities**

Employees are responsible for protecting company information and reporting suspected security incidents promptly.

Failure to follow information security requirements may result in corrective or disciplinary action.

Before separation, employees must return company property assigned to them.

This may include:

- Laptop
- Mobile device
- Access card
- Documents
- Storage devices
- Other company equipment

Access to company systems may be disabled as part of the separation process.

**18.8 Exit Interview**

Nexora may request departing employees to participate in an exit interview.

The purpose is to understand the employee's experience and identify opportunities for organizational improvement.

Participation requirements may vary depending on the circumstances.

**18.9 Final Settlement**

The final settlement may include applicable:

- Salary up to the final working date
- Approved reimbursements
- Eligible leave encashment
- Applicable variable compensation
- Statutory payments
- Authorized recoveries or deductions

The timing of final settlement depends on completion of required exit procedures and applicable legal requirements.

**18.10 Employment Documents**

After completing the required separation process, eligible employees may receive applicable employment documents such as:

- Relieving documentation
- Experience or service certificate
- Final settlement statement
- Tax or payroll-related documents

Employees should ensure that their personal contact information remains updated so that required documents can be delivered after separation.

**18.11 Termination by the Company**

Nexora may terminate employment in accordance with the employee's employment agreement, company policies, and applicable law.

Termination may occur due to reasons including:

- Serious misconduct
- Persistent performance concerns
- Business restructuring
- Role elimination
- Other legitimate employment-related reasons

Applicable notice, compensation, and procedural requirements will be determined according to the relevant circumstances.

**18.12 Abandonment of Employment**

Failure to report to work or communicate with the company for an extended period without authorization may be treated as abandonment of employment, subject to applicable law and company procedures.

The company may attempt to contact the employee before taking appropriate action.

**18.13 Post-Employment Responsibilities**

Certain obligations may continue after employment ends.

These may include obligations relating to:

- Confidentiality
- Intellectual property
- Return of company property
- Protection of company and client information
- Other contractual obligations

Employees should review their employment agreement for obligations that continue after separation.

**19. Frequently Asked Questions**

**19.1 How many Annual Leave days do I receive?**

Eligible regular employees receive **18 days of Annual Leave per calendar year** . Employees joining during the year receive a prorated entitlement.

**19.2 Can I carry forward unused Annual Leave?**

Yes. Up to **10 unused Annual Leave days** may normally be carried forward to the following calendar year.

**19.3 What happens to unused Casual Leave?**

Unused Casual Leave normally expires at the end of the calendar year and cannot normally be carried forward.

**19.4 Do I need approval before taking leave?**

Yes. Planned leave should be submitted through the designated HR system and approved before the leave begins.

**19.5 What happens if I forget to mark attendance?**

You should submit an attendance correction request through the designated system, normally within **two working days** of the missed entry.

**19.6 What are the standard working hours?**

The standard schedule is **9:30 AM to 6:30 PM, Monday to Friday** , unless a different schedule applies to your role.

**19.7 Is there a grace period for late arrival?**

Yes. A **15-minute grace period** is provided for occasional delays. For the standard schedule, arrival by 9:45 AM falls within the grace period.

The grace period is not intended to become a regular revised start time.

**19.8 Can I work from home when I am sick?**

If you are unwell but still able to work, you may request temporary Work From Home where your role permits it.

If you are unable to work, you should use the applicable Sick Leave process.

**19.9 Can I work remotely from another country?**

Not without prior written approval. International remote work may require additional review for tax, immigration, security, legal, and client-related requirements.

**19.10 Can I install software on my company laptop?**

Only authorized software should be installed. Use the company's approved software catalogue or IT support process when requesting new software.

**19.11 What should I do if I lose my company laptop?**

Report the loss immediately to the IT or Information Security team. Do not delay reporting the incident.

**19.12 Can I accept a gift from a vendor?**

Modest and customary non-cash gifts may be acceptable when they do not influence business decisions and comply with company policy.

Cash and cash-equivalent gifts must not be accepted from vendors or business partners.

**19.13 How do I report harassment?**

You may report harassment to your reporting manager, People &amp; Culture, the designated Ethics or Compliance contact, or another applicable confidential reporting channel.

**19.14 Can I report a concern anonymously?**

Where a confidential or anonymous reporting channel is available, employees may use that channel subject to its operating rules.

**19.15 How often are performance reviews conducted?**

Formal performance reviews are generally conducted **twice each year** , with informal feedback provided throughout the year.

**19.16 Can I get reimbursed for a professional certification?**

You may request reimbursement for an approved certification that is relevant to your role, subject to eligibility, prior approval, budget, and applicable reimbursement requirements.

**19.17 What is the notice period?**

Your notice period is determined by your employment agreement. Different requirements may apply during probation or for different employment categories.

**19.18 Can I leave before completing my notice period?**

You may request an early release, but approval is subject to business requirements, your employment agreement, and company procedures.

**19.19 What happens to my benefits after resignation?**

Some benefits may continue until your final working date, while others may end earlier according to the applicable benefit plan. Contact People &amp; Culture for details regarding your specific benefits.

**19.20 Who should I contact if I cannot find an answer in this handbook?**

Contact the **People &amp; Culture Department** through the designated employee support channel. For specialized matters, you may be directed to Payroll, IT, Finance, Information Security, or another appropriate team.
